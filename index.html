Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act

A transformative rights-based paradigm legalizing the "Right to Work" and securing livelihood sustainability across rural India.

What is MGNREGA?

Enacted in September 2005, the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) stands as one of the largest social safety nets globally.


Unlike conventional supply-led employment initiatives, MGNREGA operates on a statutory, demand-driven framework. It guarantees baseline economic security by offering up to 100 days of unskilled manual labor per fiscal year to any rural household with willing adult volunteers.

Vision & Core Objectives

Social Protection & Entitlement

Guarantees a statutory legal fallback structure for vulnerable, under-employed communities to counter unexpected systemic fiscal shocks.

Asset-Driven Rural Growth

Focuses structural output directly on high-yield, durable local resources—including watershed maintenance, canal digging, and rural road networks.

Decentralized Democratization

Empowers grassroots Planning units through Panchayati Raj Institutions, letting the Gram Sabha directly map local employment needs and shelf-of-works.

Salient Features of the Scheme

Legal Enforcement Safeguard

Mandates that employment must be granted within 15 days of formal application. If the local state infrastructure delays allocation, an Unemployment Allowance becomes legally payable.

Proximity Metrics

Guarantees assignments within a 5-kilometer radius of the recipient's home. Cross-boundary exceptions attract a mandatory 10% premium wage supplement.

Gender Equity Mandate

Enforces strict gender wage parity alongside a legal requirement allocating at least one-third of all active roles exclusively to women workforce participants.

Accountability Matrix

Explicitly bans external commercial contractors and high-displacement heavy machinery to ensure structural expenditure prioritizes manual, human labor.

Water Conservation & Harvesting

Prioritizes rejuvenating traditional water bodies, building check dams, and digging silt-retarding storage tanks to recharge drying rural aquifers.
